# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**  
_(Prompt B)_

---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
_(Reason 1: Prompt B breaks the task into explicit, numbered steps (identify low scores → extract themes → summarize), which makes it much easier for an AI to follow a clear sequence rather than trying to satisfy several requests bundled into one long sentence.)_

**Reason 2:**  
_(Prompt B separates out the role, audience, and constraints (concise, no jargon) as distinct labeled fields, so the AI has unambiguous guardrails for tone and format instead of having to infer them from context buried in a paragraph)_

---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
_(One strength of Prompt A is that it reads naturally and gives helpful real-world context (marketing manager, CEO presentation, campaign background) in a conversational way, which can help the AI understand the purpose behind the request, not just the mechanical steps.)_

---

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

Steps:

Summarise the findings in a short executive summary paragraph.

**What you borrowed and why:**

This keeps the clarity and step-by-step precision of Prompt B while restoring the human context and motivation from Prompt A — giving the AI both a clear task structure and enough real-world grounding to tailor its tone and priorities appropriately.)_
